## 1. Objective

Step 23 investigates the semantic relationship between:

- `Admission`
- `AdmissionDecision`
- `Orchestration`
- `Orchestration Identity`
- `Orchestration Event`
- `Orchestration Path`

The preceding steps established that:

- `ExecutionRequest` is distinct from `Execution`.
- Admission is a meaningful conceptual boundary.
- Admission Identity is representable but has not been demonstrated as mandatory.
- Request lifecycle does not require an intrinsic `RequestState`.
- Pre-execution orchestration should not be confused with execution outcome semantics.

Step 23 therefore investigates a more specific question:

> Is Admission an intrinsic semantic occurrence within the execution model, or is Admission Decision better understood as information produced or carried by pre-execution Orchestration?

The purpose is not to choose an implementation prematurely, but to identify the smallest semantic model supported by experimental evidence.

---

# 2. Research Question

> **Is Admission a semantic occurrence that belongs intrinsically to the execution model, or is Admission actually a decision produced by Orchestration?**

Two conceptual models were investigated.

### Model A — Admission as Semantic Occurrence

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
   Admission
       ↓
   Decision
       ↓
   Execution
```

### Model B — Admission as Orchestration Decision

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
 Orchestration
       └── AdmissionDecision
                 ↓
              Execution
```

A third minimal representation was also repeatedly tested:

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

---

# 3. Hypothesis H23

> **Admission Decision merupakan hasil dari suatu pre-execution decision process, tetapi belum tentu merupakan bagian intrinsik dari Execution semantics.**

More specifically:

1. Admission and Orchestration may be conceptually distinguishable.
2. Admission Identity may be distinguishable from Orchestration Identity.
3. Admission Event may be distinguishable from Orchestration Event.
4. Multiple orchestration paths may exist without becoming part of Admission semantics.
5. Admission Decision may preserve its meaning independently of orchestration context.
6. Orchestration may therefore be modeled as contextual/process information rather than as a mandatory execution semantic primitive.

The hypothesis does not assume that any of these distinctions require production classes or objects.

---

# 5. Step 23-1 — Admission Decision Ownership Probe

## Research Question

> Can the same Admission Decision semantics be represented either as an Admission-oriented observation or as an Orchestration-oriented observation?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

## Findings

The experiment demonstrated that the same decision can be represented through:

```text
AdmissionObservation
        ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

or:

```text
OrchestrationObservation
        ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

The same `ExecutionRequest` can therefore be represented in both models.

A decision does not require an execution identity.

A rejected admission can exist while no execution occurrence exists.

## Architectural Finding

The experiment demonstrates **representational viability of both models**, but does not establish ownership.

Therefore:

> **23-1 does not prove that Orchestration owns Admission.**

It only establishes that the same decision semantics can be carried by either an Admission-oriented or Orchestration-oriented representation.

# 6. Step 23-2 — Admission vs Orchestration Identity

## Research Question

> If Admission is represented as an occurrence, does it require an identity distinct from the identity of Orchestration?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

## Findings

The experimental model successfully represented:

```text
Request Identity
        ≠
Orchestration Identity
        ≠
Admission Identity
```

The model also represented multiple Admission occurrences associated with the same request and multiple Orchestration occurrences associated with the same request.

However, a minimal model without `admission_id` continued to preserve the principal Admission semantics.

Therefore:

> **Admission Identity is representable and conceptually distinguishable, but it has not been demonstrated as a mandatory semantic primitive.**

This reinforces the finding from Step 22:

> **Semantic occurrence does not automatically require a dedicated identity field.**

# 7. Step 23-3 — Admission vs Orchestration Event

## Research Question

> Is Admission an event distinct from an Orchestration Event, or can Admission Decision simply be information carried by an Orchestration Event?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

## Findings

The experiment successfully represented:

```text
Orchestration Event
        ≠
Admission Event
```

It was also possible to represent:

```text
Orchestration Event
        ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

and:

```text
ExecutionRequest
        ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

without introducing a dedicated Admission Event.

Therefore:

> **Admission Event is a viable conceptual representation, but its necessity has not been demonstrated.**

The experiment reinforces the principle:

> **Semantic distinction does not automatically require an implementation event abstraction.**

# 8. Step 23-4 — Multiple Orchestration Paths

## Research Question

> If one ExecutionRequest can be processed through multiple Orchestration paths, does Admission Decision remain semantically independent of those paths?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

## Experimental Paths

The experiment used representative paths:

```text
DIRECT
QUEUED
DEFERRED
```

The same request could be represented through different paths.

Different paths could also produce different decision observations.

The model demonstrated:

```text
ExecutionRequest
      │
      ├── Direct Path
      │
      ├── Queued Path
      │
      └── Deferred Path
             │
             ▼
      Admission Decision
```

However, the minimal representation:

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
AdmissionDecision
```

continued to preserve the principal decision semantics without path information.

## Architectural Finding

> **Orchestration Path ≠ Admission Decision.**

Multiple orchestration paths do not automatically become part of Admission semantics.

Therefore:

> **The existence of multiple orchestration paths does not establish the need for an AdmissionPath or equivalent production abstraction.**

---

# 9. Step 23-5 — Admission Decision Independence

## Research Question

> Does Admission Decision have semantic meaning independent of the Orchestration mechanism that produced or observed it?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

## Findings

Admission Decision remained representable when:

- orchestration path changed,
- orchestration details were removed,
- orchestration observation was absent,
- only the decision itself remained.

Conceptually:

```text
Orchestration Context
        │
        ▼
Admission Decision
```

could be reduced to:

```text
Admission Decision
```

without changing the value or meaning of the decision in the experimental model.

## Architectural Finding

> **Admission Decision is semantic information independent of Orchestration context.**

This does not mean that Orchestration is irrelevant.

Orchestration may still explain:

- how the decision was obtained,
- which path was used,
- which pre-execution process occurred,
- or which contextual observations accompanied the decision.

However, those contextual details do not define the semantic value of the decision itself.

# 10. Step 23-6 — Orchestration Minimality Probe

## Research Question

> How much Orchestration information is actually required to represent Admission Decision semantics?

## Result

```text
12/12 PASS
```

Three representations were compared:

### Model A

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
Orchestration
       ↓
Admission
       ↓
Decision
```

### Model B

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
Orchestration
       ↓
Decision
```

### Model C

```text
ExecutionRequest
       ↓
Decision
```

All three were able to preserve the principal Admission Decision semantics.

The minimal model did not require:

- Orchestration identity
- Orchestration event
- Orchestration path
- Admission identity
- Admission event

## Architectural Finding

> **Orchestration is not required to represent the minimum semantic information of Admission Decision.**

This is the strongest evidence of Step 23.

It indicates that Orchestration should currently be treated as contextual/process semantics rather than automatically promoted into a mandatory execution-model primitive.

# 12. Consolidated Architectural Findings

Step 23 establishes the following findings.

## 12.1 Admission Decision is semantically distinguishable

`AdmissionDecision` is meaningful information concerning whether an `ExecutionRequest` may proceed toward execution.

It is distinct from:

```text
ExecutionRequest
Execution
ExecutionState
ExecutionResult
```

---

## 12.2 Admission Decision does not intrinsically require Orchestration

The decision can be represented without preserving the process that produced it.

Therefore:

```text
Orchestration
       ≠
Admission Decision
```

---

## 12.3 Orchestration is contextual/process semantics

Orchestration may explain the circumstances surrounding an Admission Decision.

Examples include:

```text
Direct
Queued
Deferred
Reconsidered
Cancelled
```

But these observations do not define the semantic identity of the decision itself.

---

## 12.4 Admission Identity is optional at the semantic level

A dedicated:

```text
admission_id
```

is conceptually possible.

However, no experiment in Step 23 demonstrated that the identity is required to preserve the semantics already discovered.

Therefore it remains an implementation option, not an established semantic primitive.

---

## 12.5 Admission Event is optional at the semantic level

Likewise:

```text
AdmissionEvent
```

is a viable conceptual model.

But the experiments demonstrate that Admission Decision can be represented without such an event.

Therefore no production Admission Event abstraction is currently justified by evidence alone.

---

## 12.6 Orchestration Path is not Admission semantics

Different orchestration paths can coexist while the decision semantics remain independently representable.

Therefore:

> **Orchestration Path ≠ Admission Decision**

---

## 12.7 Semantic information should not be confused with its production context

A central finding of Step 23 is:

```text
Decision
    ≠
Process that produced Decision
```

The process may be important operationally, but it does not necessarily belong to the semantic definition of the decision.

# 14. Updated Conceptual Architecture

The current conceptual model can therefore remain:

```text
Operation
    ↓
ExecutionRequest
    │
    ├── Pre-execution / Orchestration Context
    │        │
    │        ├── Admission Decision
    │        ├── Queue / Deferred observations
    │        └── Other orchestration observations
    │
    └── Execution
             ↓
       ExecutionResult
```

The semantic core may be represented minimally as:

```text
Operation
    ↓
ExecutionRequest
    ↓
AdmissionDecision
    ↓
Execution
    ↓
ExecutionResult
```

The relationship between the two should be understood as:

```text
Orchestration
     │
     └── may produce / carry / explain
                    ↓
             AdmissionDecision
```

rather than:

```text
Orchestration
     ↓
mandatory semantic layer
     ↓
Admission primitive
```

# 15. Core Principles Consolidated

Step 23 reinforces the following principles.

### Principle 1 — Decision ≠ Decision Process

The semantic meaning of a decision should not automatically include the process that produced it.

### Principle 2 — Orchestration ≠ Execution Semantics

Pre-execution orchestration should not be confused with execution outcome semantics.

### Principle 3 — Semantic Boundary ≠ Implementation Abstraction

A meaningful conceptual distinction does not automatically require a dedicated production class, object, event, or identity.

### Principle 4 — Context ≠ Core Semantic Identity

Path, event, identity, and orchestration context may provide useful information without defining the core semantic meaning.

### Principle 5 — Minimal Representation is a Valid Architectural Probe

If a semantic concept remains representable after contextual information is removed, that contextual information has not yet been demonstrated as intrinsically necessary.

---

# 16. Non-Findings

Step 23 does **not** establish the following:

- that Orchestration is unnecessary in the complete application architecture;
- that asynchronous orchestration is unnecessary;
- that queues or schedulers are unnecessary;
- that Admission should never become a production object;
- that Admission Decision must be a primitive enum;
- that event sourcing should or should not be used;
- that orchestration should belong to Core Engine or Application layer;
- that Admission cannot later acquire an identity;
- that Admission cannot later become a first-class domain concept;
- that all future capabilities will require identical orchestration semantics.

These remain open architectural questions.

# 19. Limitations

The experiments are intentionally simplified.

They do not yet model:

- concurrent requests;
- asynchronous execution;
- scheduler ownership;
- queue persistence;
- timeout semantics;
- distributed orchestration;
- authorization policies;
- resource admission;
- priority;
- capacity constraints;
- transactional boundaries;
- external workflow engines.

Therefore the findings apply specifically to the semantic boundary investigated in Phase 2E-5.

They should not be generalized into a complete orchestration architecture.

---

# 20. Open Questions

The following remain available for future research:

1. Does Admission interact with authorization or capability availability?
2. Can one request receive multiple Admission Decisions over time without implying mutation of the request?
3. How should reconsideration interact with execution occurrence?
4. Does asynchronous execution introduce semantic requirements not visible in the current synchronous model?
5. Where should orchestration semantics belong architecturally?
6. Is orchestration merely Application-layer behavior, or can some generic orchestration semantics belong in Core?
7. Does cancellation require a richer relationship between Request, Orchestration, Admission, and Execution?
8. What happens when multiple orchestration agents independently evaluate the same request?
9. Does concurrency introduce a semantic identity requirement that the current experiments have not exposed?

These questions should be investigated only when they become relevant to the research progression.

The current architectural decision is:

> **Treat Admission Decision as semantically distinguishable information associated with an ExecutionRequest, while treating Orchestration as contextual/pre-execution process semantics that may produce, carry, or explain that decision.**

No dedicated Admission or Orchestration production abstraction is mandated at this stage.

The conceptual distinction is retained for further research, while implementation abstraction remains intentionally deferred.

> **Admission Decision is semantically independent from the Orchestration process that produces or observes it.**
>
> **Admission, Admission Identity, Admission Event, Orchestration Identity, Orchestration Event, and Orchestration Path are all representable conceptual distinctions, but none has been demonstrated by Step 23 to be a mandatory semantic primitive.**
>
> **The smallest experimentally validated representation remains:**
>
> ```text
> ExecutionRequest
>        ↓
> AdmissionDecision
>        ↓
> Execution
> ```
>
> **Orchestration may exist around this model as contextual/process semantics without becoming part of the intrinsic execution semantic core.**
