## 2. Research Objective

Penelitian ini bertujuan menginvestigasi batas semantik antara Context Identity, Composition Identity, Membership, dan Membership Occurrence.

Penelitian berangkat dari temuan Step 42 bahwa Composition Membership dan Relationship Endpoint Participation merupakan semantic roles yang berbeda.

Step 43 mempersempit fokus pada keanggotaan Context dalam Composition dan menguji apakah:

1. Identitas Context berbeda dari keanggotaannya.
2. Perubahan keanggotaan dapat terjadi tanpa mengubah Context Identity.
3. Keanggotaan dalam satu Composition dapat berubah tanpa mengharuskan perubahan keanggotaan dalam Composition lain.
4. Keanggotaan yang dihapus lalu dibentuk kembali dapat dibedakan sebagai occurrence terpisah apabila kebutuhan domain memerlukan pembedaan historis.

Penelitian ini tidak menetapkan bahwa setiap sistem wajib mempunyai Membership ID tersendiri.

## 3. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question

Can Context Identity, Composition Membership, and Membership Occurrence be treated as distinct conceptual concerns?

### Supporting Questions

1. Can the same Context belong to multiple Compositions?
2. Can one membership be removed while Context Identity remains unchanged?
3. Can one membership be added without changing existing memberships?
4. Can a membership association be absent and later re-established?
5. Is the pair `(Context Identity, Composition Identity)` sufficient to distinguish separate membership occurrences when historical differentiation is required?

## 4. Conceptual Definitions

### 4.1 Context Identity

Context Identity identifies a Context independently of its membership in any particular Composition.

A Context's identity does not, by itself, specify which Compositions contain that Context.

### 4.2 Composition Identity

Composition Identity identifies a Composition independently of the Contexts currently included in it.

The membership of a Composition may change without necessarily changing its identity.

This is a property of the experimental model, not a universal identity rule for all possible implementations.

### 4.3 Composition Membership

Composition Membership represents the association between a Context and a Composition.

For example:

`(context-A, composition-1)`

represents the membership of `context-A` in `composition-1`.

The same Context may have membership associations with different Compositions.

### 4.4 Membership Occurrence

Membership Occurrence refers to a particular occurrence of a membership association when the domain requires separate occurrences to be distinguishable.

Consider the following sequence:

1. `context-A` belongs to `composition-1`.
2. The membership is removed.
3. `context-A` later belongs to `composition-1` again.

The Context and Composition identities may be identical across the sequence.

Whether the later association is treated as the same logical membership or as a new occurrence depends on the identity contract and historical requirements of the domain.

### 4.5 Identity and Representation

A conceptual distinction does not require a dedicated runtime object, database table, or standalone identifier for every concept.

Membership may be represented by a pair of identities when that representation satisfies the domain requirements.

Additional occurrence information may be necessary when the model must distinguish separate historical instances of the same association.

## 5. Hypotheses

H1. Context Identity and Composition Membership are distinct conceptual concerns.

H2. The same Context may belong to multiple Compositions in the experimental model.

H3. Removing one membership does not intrinsically require changing Context Identity.

H4. Removing one membership does not intrinsically require removing other memberships of the same Context.

H5. Adding one membership does not intrinsically require changing Context Identity or existing memberships.

H6. The pair `(Context Identity, Composition Identity)` does not distinguish separate occurrences of the same association if the pair is identical before and after a removal and re-addition.

H7. Whether Membership Occurrence requires a distinct identifier depends on the identity contract and the need to preserve historical distinctions.

## 7. Experimental Model

The experiments represent:

- Context through a Context Identity.
- Composition through a Composition Identity and a list of members.
- Membership through a Context–Composition association.
- Membership absence through the absence of that association in the relevant model state.

The fourth experiment represents membership before and after an intervening absence using the same Context and Composition identities.

It intentionally uses the same association representation at both points.

Consequently, the experiment can demonstrate that the pair alone does not encode a distinction between those two occurrences.

It does not determine which additional representation should be adopted by a production system.

## 13. Consolidated Architectural Findings

### 13.1 Context Identity Is Distinct from Membership

Context Identity identifies the Context independently of any particular Composition membership.

Membership represents the association between a Context and a Composition.

### 13.2 Membership Changes Do Not Intrinsically Determine Context Identity Changes

The experiments demonstrate that membership can be added or removed while Context Identity remains stable.

This is a supported modeling possibility, not a universal requirement for all implementations.

### 13.3 Membership Associations Can Be Distinguished by Composition

The same Context can belong to multiple Compositions.

The Context–Composition pair can distinguish current associations with different Compositions.

### 13.4 Current Association and Historical Occurrence Are Different Concerns

A current membership representation answers whether a Context is associated with a Composition in the model.

Historical occurrence distinction answers whether two temporally separate instances of the same association should be treated as different occurrences.

The latter requirement cannot be satisfied by the Context–Composition pair alone when the pair is identical across both occurrences.

### 13.5 A Dedicated Membership ID Is Not Universally Required

The experiments do not justify imposing a separate Membership ID on every system.

Possible representation choices depend on requirements:

- Current membership only: a Context–Composition pair may be sufficient.
- Historical differentiation: additional occurrence information may be required.
- Explicit membership identity: a dedicated identifier may be appropriate when justified by the domain's identity contract.

These are alternative modeling possibilities, not implementation decisions made by this research.

### 13.6 Occurrence Distinction Does Not Automatically Define Lifecycle Semantics

Distinguishing historical occurrences does not, by itself, define lifecycle states, transition rules, retention policy, or deletion behavior.

Those concerns require separate research if the domain needs them.

## 14. Limitations

The following limitations apply:

1. The experiments use deliberately constructed in-memory dictionaries.
2. The tests verify the properties of the supplied fixtures rather than a production implementation.
3. The experiments do not evaluate database persistence or historical event storage.
4. The experiments do not define a universal Membership Identity scheme.
5. The experiments do not determine whether a re-established membership must receive a new identifier.
6. The experiments do not define lifecycle, retention, deletion, permission, or concurrency policies.
7. The experiments do not prove that membership constraints are absent from specific domains.
8. The experiments do not require separate runtime classes, database tables, or identifiers for each conceptual concern.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as conceptual model evidence.

## 15. Final Architectural Principle

**Context Identity, Composition Membership, and Membership Occurrence are distinct conceptual concerns.**

Context Identity identifies the Context independently of its memberships.

Composition Membership represents an association between a Context and a Composition.

Membership Occurrence distinguishes a particular occurrence of that association when the domain requires historical differentiation.

The pair `(Context Identity, Composition Identity)` can represent a current association, but it cannot by itself distinguish separate occurrences of the same association when that association is removed and later re-established.

Whether additional occurrence information or a dedicated Membership ID is required depends on the domain's identity contract and historical requirements.

### Compact Principle

- `Context Identity ≠ Membership`
- `Membership Removal ≠ Context Removal`
- `Membership Addition ≠ Context Identity Change`
- `Current Association ≠ Historical Occurrence`
- `Occurrence Distinction ≠ Mandatory Dedicated Membership ID`
