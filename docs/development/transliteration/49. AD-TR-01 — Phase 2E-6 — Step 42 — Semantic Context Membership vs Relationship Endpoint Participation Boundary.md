## 2. Research Objective

Penelitian ini bertujuan menentukan apakah Composition Membership dan Relationship Endpoint Participation merupakan dua semantic roles yang berbeda.

Penelitian berfokus pada batas konseptual antara:

- Membership suatu Context di dalam Composition.
- Partisipasi suatu Context sebagai source atau target dari Relationship.

Penelitian juga menguji apakah perubahan pada kedua peran tersebut dapat direpresentasikan secara terpisah, baik ketika hanya salah satunya berubah maupun ketika keduanya berubah dalam satu transisi.

Penelitian ini tidak mengimplementasikan production architecture dan tidak menetapkan aturan universal untuk seluruh domain.

## 3. Research Question

**Primary Research Question**

Can Composition Membership and Relationship Endpoint Participation be treated as distinct semantic roles, such that membership does not intrinsically determine endpoint participation, and endpoint participation does not intrinsically determine membership?

**Supporting Questions**

1. Can membership exist without endpoint participation in the Relationship under examination?
2. Can endpoint participation exist without membership in the Composition under examination?
3. Can membership change while Relationship endpoints remain unchanged?
4. Can Relationship endpoints change while membership remains unchanged?
5. Can membership and endpoints change together without becoming semantically identical?

## 4. Conceptual Definitions

### 4.1 Composition Membership

Composition Membership describes whether a Context is included as a member of a particular Composition.

Membership is evaluated relative to a Composition identity.

A Context's membership in one Composition does not, by itself, establish its membership in another Composition.

### 4.2 Relationship Endpoint Participation

Relationship Endpoint Participation describes the role of a Context as an endpoint of a particular Relationship.

In the experimental model, a Relationship has two explicitly represented endpoint roles:

- Source
- Target

Endpoint participation is evaluated relative to a Relationship identity and its endpoint configuration.

### 4.3 Semantic Distinction

Membership and endpoint participation describe different semantic roles.

A Context can be:

- A member without participating as an endpoint in the Relationship under examination.
- An endpoint without being a member of the Composition under examination.
- Both a member and an endpoint.
- Neither a member nor an endpoint.

These combinations are conceptual possibilities within the experimental model. Whether a specific combination is valid in a real domain depends on that domain's explicit constraints.

## 5. Hypotheses

The experiments evaluate the following hypotheses:

H1. Composition Membership and Relationship Endpoint Participation can be represented as distinct semantic roles.

H2. Membership does not intrinsically determine endpoint participation.

H3. Endpoint participation does not intrinsically determine membership.

H4. Membership can change while Relationship endpoints remain unchanged.

H5. Relationship endpoints can change while Composition Membership remains unchanged.

H6. Membership and endpoint changes can coexist without becoming semantically identical.

H7. Composition Identity and Relationship Identity can remain stable across the tested changes when the experimental model defines identity independently of membership and endpoint values.

## 7. Experimental Model

The experiments represent Composition and Relationship as separate structures.

A Composition contains:

- An identity.
- A list of Context members.

A Relationship contains:

- An identity.
- A source endpoint.
- A target endpoint.

The experimental model intentionally represents membership and endpoints as separate fields.

The model does not automatically synchronize Composition membership with Relationship endpoints.

This separation permits independent changes to be represented and inspected.

It does not imply that every production system must use separate classes, database tables, objects, or identifiers for these concepts.

## 13. Architectural Findings

### 13.1 Membership and Endpoint Participation Are Distinct

Composition Membership describes inclusion within a Composition.

Relationship Endpoint Participation describes a Context's role as an endpoint of a Relationship.

The two concepts have different semantic referents and should not be treated as interchangeable merely because they may refer to the same Context.

### 13.2 Neither Role Intrinsically Determines the Other

Membership alone does not establish endpoint participation in a particular Relationship.

Endpoint participation alone does not establish membership in a particular Composition.

A domain may impose additional constraints connecting the two, but those constraints must be explicit rather than inferred from the concepts themselves.

### 13.3 Changes Can Be Represented Independently

The experiments demonstrate three modeled situations:

1. Membership changes while endpoints remain stable.
2. Endpoints change while membership remains stable.
3. Both change together.

These situations support independent representation of the two semantic roles.

### 13.4 Coexistence Does Not Imply Semantic Collapse

Membership and endpoint participation may refer to the same Context and may change in the same transition.

Neither shared referents nor simultaneous changes make the roles identical.

### 13.5 Identity Is a Separate Modeling Concern

The experimental model permits Composition identity to remain stable when membership changes and Relationship identity to remain stable when endpoints change.

These results reinforce the distinction between semantic role and identity.

They do not establish a universal identity policy. Whether a change creates a new identity depends on the identity contract defined for the relevant entity or domain.

### 13.6 Semantic Independence Does Not Exclude Explicit Constraints

A production system may define constraints such as requiring every Relationship endpoint to belong to a specified Composition.

Such a constraint would create an explicit domain-specific dependency.

It would not make Membership and Endpoint Participation synonymous. It would define a rule governing their valid combinations.

## 14. Limitations

The following limitations apply:

1. The experiments use deliberately constructed in-memory dictionaries.
2. The tests verify properties of the supplied fixtures rather than a production implementation.
3. The tests do not explore every possible combination of Composition members and Relationship endpoints.
4. The experiments do not implement or evaluate domain-specific membership constraints.
5. The experiments do not establish universal causal independence.
6. The tests do not establish universal identity stability under membership or endpoint changes.
7. The experiments do not require separate runtime classes, database tables, or physical identifiers for each semantic role.
8. No performance, persistence, concurrency, or integration behavior is evaluated.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as conceptual model evidence.

## 15. Final Architectural Principle

**Composition Membership and Relationship Endpoint Participation are distinct semantic roles.**

Membership describes the inclusion of a Context within a Composition, while endpoint participation describes the role of a Context as a source or target of a Relationship.

In the tested model, these roles can be represented independently, can change separately, and can change together without becoming semantically identical.

Neither role intrinsically determines the identity, existence, or change of the other. Domain-specific constraints may introduce explicit dependencies between them without collapsing their semantic distinction.

### Compact Principle

- `Membership ≠ Endpoint Participation`
- `Membership Change ≠ Endpoint Change`
- `Independent Representation ≠ Universal Absence of Dependency`
- `Coexistence ≠ Semantic Collapse`
